### Slide 4
- Solid-State Batteries
- Solid-state batteries are a type of rechargeable battery that uses a solid electrolyte instead of a liquid or gel one, offering potential advantages like increased safety, higher energy density, and faster charging
- This technology is seen as a promising alternative to traditional lithium-ion batteries, particularly for electric vehicles
- 4
- Figure 1: Solid state battery [1]

### Slide 5
- Components of Solid-State Batteries
- Cathode:
- The positive electrode, often made of lithium metal oxides (like LiCoO2, LiNiMnCoO2, or LiFePO4), is responsible for providing lithium ions during discharge.
- Anode:
- The negative electrode, traditionally made of lithium metal (pure lithium), is responsible for storing and releasing lithium ions during the charging and discharging process.
- Solid Electrolyte/Separator:
- This component replaces the liquid electrolyte and separator in traditional batteries. It facilitates the movement of lithium ions between the anode and cathode while also providing mechanical separation between them

- Electronic Devices
- Improving the performance of electronic devices while minimizing energy loss involves a combination of technological advancements and strategic design choices.
- Key areas include using high-efficiency components, optimizing circuit design, employing advanced power management techniques, and exploring new materials and architectures.

### Slide 5
- Energy loss in electronic devices
- Resistive Losses:
- The flow of current through any component with resistance (like wires, resistors) results in power loss as heat
- Switching Losses:
- In electronic circuits that switch between on and off states (like transistors), the energy lost during the switching process is known as switching loss
- Leakage Currents:
- Even when a device is supposed to be off, there can be small leakage currents that dissipate energy
- Core Losses (in transformers and inductors):
- In devices like transformers and inductors, energy can be lost in the magnetic core due to phenomena like eddy currents and hysteresis
- Thermal Losses:
- Energy lost as heat due to various factors like resistance and switching can lead to increased operating temperatures, which can affect performance and lifespan

### Slide 7
- Design:
- Improved Circuit Design
- Sub-threshold Circuit Design: Designing circuits that operate in the sub-threshold region, where the voltage is below the threshold for traditional logic gates, can lead to extremely low power consumption.
- Adiabatic Logic: This involves using energy recovery techniques during the switching of circuit states, which can significantly reduce energy loss in digital logic circuits.
- Power Gating: Disconnecting parts of the circuit that are not being used to reduce standby power consumption is a common technique for improving energy efficiency.

### Slide 8
- Improving the performance of electronic devices:Strategies
- Improving the performance of electronic devices while minimizing energy loss is a critical goal in modern electronics, as it not only boosts efficiency but also extends the longevity of devices and reduces environmental impact. Here are several strategies that can be employed to achieve are explained below:
- Low Power Design
- Power-Efficient Materials
- Energy Harvesting
- Efficient Power Management

### Slide 9
- Low Power Design
- :
- Use of Low Power Components: Selecting components such as low-voltage transistors, low-power microcontrollers, and low-power logic gates can reduce energy consumption.
- Clock Gating: This technique involves turning off the clock signal to portions of the device when they are not in use. This reduces unnecessary power consumption in idle states.
- Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling (DVFS): Adjusting the voltage and frequency according to the workload can reduce power consumption when the device is not under heavy load.

### Slide 10
- Power-Efficient Materials
- Graphene and Carbon Nanotubes: These materials exhibit excellent electrical conductivity and minimal heat loss, improving performance while reducing energy waste.
- Wide-Bandgap Semiconductors: Materials such as Gallium Nitride (GaN) and Silicon Carbide (SiC) allow devices to operate at higher temperatures and voltages, reducing energy loss in high-power applications.
- Superconducting Materials: Superconductors have zero electrical resistance at low temperatures, allowing for energy-efficient electronic components in specialized applications (e.g., MRI machines, quantum computing).

### Slide 11
- Energy Harvesting:
- Ambient Energy Sources: Energy harvesting techniques, such as capturing vibrations, thermal energy, or even solar power, can be used to power electronic devices without relying solely on external power sources.
- Piezoelectric and Thermoelectric Devices: These devices convert mechanical stress or heat gradients into electrical energy, making them useful in reducing dependence on batteries for small devices or sensors.
- 11

### Slide 12
- Efficient Power Management
- Power Management ICs (PMICs): These integrated circuits are designed to manage power distribution and reduce energy loss through optimized voltage regulation, reducing inefficiencies in power conversion.
- Energy-Efficient Power Supplies: Switching power supplies are more efficient than traditional linear power supplies, as they convert power with minimal loss, especially in high-power applications.

### Slide 13
- New materials in reducing power loss
- High-Temperature Superconductors (HTS):
- HTS materials offer the potential for zero-resistance electricity transmission, significantly reducing I²R losses

- Soft Magnetic Materials:
- Soft magnetic materials, like soft magnetic composite materials (SMCs), are used in cores of transformers and electric motors to minimize core losses, enhancing efficiency and reducing the size of equipment.
- High-Temperature Low-Sag Conductors (HTLS):
