### Chapter description and Learning outcomes
# Chapter description and Learning outcomes

This unit introduces the fundamental concepts of Quantum Physics and Nanotechnology, which form the basis of modern scientific and technological advancements. Students will study the limitations of classical physics and understand important quantum concepts such as Planck’s hypothesis, photoelectric effect, wave-particle duality, de-Broglie matter waves, wave packets, wave functions, and Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle. The unit also covers the basics of nanotechnology, including the quantum confinement effect, classification of nanomaterials, and major synthesis techniques such as top-down (ball milling) and bottom-up (sol-gel) methods. Further, students will explore recent inventions and applications in emerging areas like quantum computing, edge computing, carbon nanomaterials, semiconductor nanodevices, green nanotechnology, nanocomposites, nanosensors, nanoencapsulation, water treatment, and energy harvesting, highlighting the role of nanotechnology in solving real-world engineering and societal challenges.

Learning Outcomes (LOs)

After completing this unit, students will be able to:

LO1: Explain the fundamental principles of quantum physics, including Planck’s theory, photoelectric effect, and wave-particle duality.

LO2: Apply concepts such as de-Broglie wavelength, uncertainty principle, and wave function to analyze microscopic physical systems.

LO3: Describe the fundamentals of nanotechnology, quantum confinement effect, and classify different types of nanomaterials.

LO4: Compare top-down and bottom-up synthesis methods and explain techniques like ball milling and sol-gel processing.

LO5: Identify and discuss recent innovations and engineering applications of nanotechnology in computing, healthcare, environment, energy, and advanced materials.

### Description and Learning Outcomes
# Description and Learning Outcomes

This unit introduces the fundamental principles of lasers and optical fiber technology. It covers the concepts of spontaneous and stimulated emission of radiation, Einstein coefficients, conditions for amplification, and the essential components of laser systems. The working and applications of Ruby Laser, Semiconductor Laser, and Holography are also discussed. The second part of the unit focuses on optical fibers, including their principle, structure, types, acceptance angle, numerical aperture, and transmission losses. The unit provides students with an understanding of modern optical communication systems and laser applications in science and engineering.

## Learning Outcomes

After studying this unit, students will be able to:

- Explain the concepts of spontaneous and stimulated emission, Einstein coefficients, and conditions for laser amplification.

- Describe the construction, working principle, and applications of Ruby Laser and Semiconductor Laser.

- Understand the principles and applications of holography and laser technology.

- Explain the principle, structure, and types of optical fibers used in communication systems.

- Analyze important fiber optic parameters such as acceptance angle, numerical aperture, and losses in optical fibers.

### Slide 15
- Quantum cryptography utilizes the principles of quantum mechanics to secure communications, particularly in key distribution and encryption. The key cryptography protocol algorithms are:
- BB84 Protocol (Quantum Key Distribution)
- It uses polarized photons to encode bits of a secret encryption key. Two parties (often called Alice and Bob) exchange photons in random polarization bases (horizontal/vertical or diagonal).
- Shor’s Algorithm (Quantum Code-Breaking)
- It can factor large integers exponentially faster than the best-known classical algorithms. Modern encryption like RSA relies on the fact that factoring very large numbers is extremely time-consuming for classical computers.
- Grover’s Algorithm (Speeding up Search)
- Search unsorted databases or solve problems where the solution must be guessed and verified (like brute-force key searching). It reduces the time complexity from O(N)to O(√N).
- Quantum Computing in Cryptography
